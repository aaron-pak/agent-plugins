# Interview

Interview the user until you both agree on what they want and why. They may already
know and want to check that you've got it, have a rough idea to flesh out, or not know yet and
find out through your questions.

Don't ask what you can look up or safely assume; say what you assumed so they can correct it.

Ask a few questions at a time, and wait for the answers before choosing the next ones, since an
answer can make a planned question moot. Start with what everything else depends on. Ask about a
detail only when its answer would change the direction now.

Reacting to something concrete is easier than answering an open question, so where you can,
offer your best guess or a few options. Every time you offer options, say which one you'd pick,
with a one-line reason. If your client has a tool for asking questions with options, use it.

Stop asking once the questions left either wouldn't change what you'd do, or would be answered
faster by starting the work. Then write back what you've agreed and ask them to confirm or
correct it.
